<p align="justify">
I am Rahul Vishwakarma, a Research Engineer at WorkOnward. Before that, I spent several years as a Senior Machine Learning Engineer at Dell Technologies, working on reliability problems in large storage systems. My research keeps circling the same question: how do we build AI systems whose predictions come with honest, measurable guarantees, especially when a wrong answer is expensive?
</p>

<p align="justify">
Most of my academic work is in trustworthy machine learning for hardware security. My master's thesis at Cal State Long Beach, funded by the NSF, treated hardware Trojan detection as an uncertainty problem rather than a pure classification one. A detector that is wrong but confident is worse than one that knows when it is unsure. That work appeared at ICCAD 2023 and DATE 2024, and in the Journal of Hardware and Systems Security. A second thread looks at chaos-based encryption, where my 2023 survey in IEEE Access has become my most-cited paper with 50 citations. The methodological backbone across both is uncertainty quantification and conformal prediction, including two papers at COPA 2023 and a short book I wrote from a practitioner's perspective. More recently I've been moving toward agentic AI and LLM systems, with work on data pipelines for fine-tuning, agent communication protocols, and LLM-based vulnerability detection.
</p>

<p align="justify">
On the industry side, I hold 64 granted U.S. patents, mostly from my Dell years, on ML-driven storage reliability like disk-failure forecasting and backup failure prevention. A few of those patent families have since been cited by later filings from Amazon, IBM, Salesforce, VMware, SAP, Micron, and Databricks, which is public record on Google Patents. I'll be straight about what that means and what it doesn't. Patent citations are wildly skewed: about a quarter of patents are never cited at all, the average inventor holds around three, and the literature calls anyone with fifteen or more prolific. Most of my patents are incremental engineering, and none of them, to my knowledge, shipped in a Dell product.
</p>
